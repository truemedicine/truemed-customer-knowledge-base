Once you have your Truemed Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN) and itemized purchase receipt, you can submit a reimbursement claim to your HSA/FSA administrator. Your LMN can cover qualifying purchases of the same type of product from the same merchant for 12 months.

This guide walks you through the reimbursement process, which should take less than five minutes to complete.

## Step-by-step reimbursement guide

### Complete your Truemed purchase and gather your documents

After your purchase is qualified through Truemed, you’ll receive two documents:

1. **A Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN)** signed by an independent licensed healthcare provider.
2. **A purchase receipt** from Truemed or, in some cases, directly from the merchant.

Both documents are required to submit a claim. You can find them in your Truemed dashboard:

- Your LMN is on the Overview page under **Download Documents**.
- Your receipt is available on the Overview page or under the individual order in the **Orders** tab.

### Identify your HSA or FSA administrator

Your administrator is the company that manages your HSA or FSA account — not your employer and not your insurance company. Common administrators include HealthEquity, Optum, Fidelity, Inspira (formerly PayFlex), WEX, HSA Bank, Lively, Navia, Ameriflex, EBC, BAS, and Basic.

If you aren't sure who your administrator is, check your benefits card, log into your benefits portal, or ask your HR team.

### Log into your HSA/FSA administrator's portal 

Most administrators have an online portal or mobile app where you can manage your account and submit claims. If you have not set up an account yet, you may need to register first.

1. **Navigate to the reimbursement or claims section.**
   Look for a section labeled something like "Submit a Claim," "Reimbursements," or "File a Claim." The exact wording varies by administrator.
2. **Upload your LMN and receipt.**
   Attach both documents to your claim. Some administrators ask you to upload them together; others have separate fields for each. Make sure the receipt clearly shows the purchase amount, merchant name, and date. See[ *Receipts*](https://help.truemed.com/reimbursements-claims/receipts) for the full requirements.
3. **Submit your claim and note the confirmation.**
   After submitting, save or screenshot any confirmation number or reference ID. This makes it easier to follow up if there is a delay.
4. **Wait for processing and receive your reimbursement.**
   Once approved, the reimbursement is deposited directly into your designated bank account or HSA/FSA account. You can reuse the same LMN for any additional qualifying purchases within the 12-month window — just submit a new receipt each time.

## Reimbursement timeline

Reimbursement timing depends on your HSA/FSA administrator. Many claims are typically processed within 7 to 10 business days after you submit your LMN and receipt, but some take longer.

Processing times can vary based on:

- **Your administrator’s processing speed.** Some administrators process claims faster than others. Employer-managed plans may have different timelines.
- **How you submitted.** Online submissions are generally processed faster than claims sent by mail.
- **Claim completeness.** If your documentation is incomplete or unclear, your administrator may pause the claim and request additional information, adding days or weeks to the process.
- **Time of year and claim volume.** Claim volumes may increase near FSA deadlines, which can slow processing.

If you haven’t heard back within two weeks of submitting your claim, follow up with your administrator directly.

## **Common reimbursement mistakes to avoid**

A few checks before submitting can help prevent delays or problems with your claim:

- **Submit an itemized receipt.** A card statement or order confirmation that only shows the total may not be sufficient. 
- **Use an LMN issued for the same merchant.** An LMN may cover qualifying purchases of the same type of product from the same merchant for 12 months.
- **Check your LMN’s validity period.** Purchases made after your LMN expires may not be covered. 
- **Complete your health survey on time.** Completing the survey after your purchase date may affect your eligibility for reimbursement. See the help center’s guidance on retroactive claims.
- **Check your FSA’s claim deadline.** FSA plans may have deadlines for submitting claims, so confirm your plan’s requirements and submit your documentation on time.
- **Submit your claim to the right organization.** Your HSA/FSA administrator manages your account and processes reimbursement claims. Your employer and health insurance company generally aren’t the right place to submit your claim.

## What to do if reimbursement is delayed

**First, check your claim status.** Log into your administrator's portal and look for the status of your submitted claim. It may be pending, under review, or waiting for additional documentation.

**If the claim is just delayed (still pending or in review):** Contact your administrator's customer support line or chat. Have your confirmation number and submission date ready. Ask if there is anything missing from your claim or an estimated resolution date.

**If the claim was denied:** A denial does not always mean the expense is ineligible. Common reasons for denial include:

- Missing or unclear documentation (resubmit with a cleaner copy of your LMN or receipt)
- The expense category was not recognized (ask your administrator to review under IRS Publication 502 eligible medical expenses)
- A technical issue with the submission

You can typically appeal a denial by contacting your administrator and providing any additional documentation they request.

**When to contact Truemed support:** If you are having trouble with your LMN (it looks incorrect, you never received it, or your administrator says it is not valid), reach out to Truemed at support@truemed.com. Truemed can resend your LMN or help clarify documentation questions. For issues with your account balance, claim status, or payment processing, your administrator is the right first contact.
